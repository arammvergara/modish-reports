Highlights & Recommendations

✓ Wins This Week

  • Paid Social revenue nearly doubled: £6,784 vs £3,489 (+94.4%) — the biggest single-week swing in recent reports, alongside sessions +10.8% and conversions +9.3%.
  • Paid Social users grew +7.1% (5,332 vs 4,977), continuing the channel's multi-week momentum.
  • Total sessions up +1.6% WoW (11,843 vs 11,660) — overall top-of-funnel volume held up well.
  • Organic Social sessions, users and conversions were all only marginally down (−0.8% to −1.6%) — a relatively stable week for the channel by volume, even with the revenue drop below.

! Areas to Investigate

  • Organic Social revenue fell to £0 from £1,121 the week before (−100.0%) — the one strong week for organic-attributed revenue didn't carry over. Check whether last week's revenue came from a single campaign or post that didn't repeat.
  • Paid Social bounce rate worsened sharply: 0.78% vs 0.55% (+41.8%) even as revenue surged — worth confirming this isn't lower-quality traffic mixed in with the volume that's driving the extra revenue.
  • Organic Social bounce rate also rose +35.0% (1.39% vs 1.03%) — a second consecutive week of bounce rate increases on this channel.
  • Total users (−0.6%) and total conversions (−0.1%) were both essentially flat despite Paid Social's strong growth — Cross-network sessions fell −17.2% (1,980 vs 2,392), which looks like the main offsetting factor.
  • Paid Search revenue was unusually high this week (£13,427, the largest single-channel revenue figure across all channels) — worth a quick sanity check that this isn't a one-off attribution spike before treating it as a new baseline.
Next Steps — Prioritised
  1. Understand the Organic Social revenue drop-off. £1,121 last week to £0 this week — identify what specifically drove last week's revenue (a post, a product, a promo) and whether it's repeatable rather than a one-off.
  2. Check Paid Social traffic quality. Bounce rate rose +41.8% alongside the revenue surge — confirm the extra revenue isn't being driven by a narrow set of high-value orders while broader traffic quality declines.
  3. Verify the Paid Search revenue figure (£13,427). This is the highest single-channel revenue this period — cross-check against Google Ads to rule out a reporting or attribution anomaly.
  4. Investigate the Cross-network decline. Sessions down −17.2% WoW — determine if this is a genuine platform drop or a GA4 attribution shift, since it's now offsetting Paid Social's gains at the total level.
  5. Keep monitoring the Organic Social bounce rate trend. Two consecutive weeks of increases (+32.1%, then +35.0%) — review recent organic post formats and landing pages for a possible mismatch.
